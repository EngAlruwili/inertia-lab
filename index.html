What happens when a power grid loses its spinning mass?

Every coal, gas and nuclear unit stores kinetic energy in a rotor spinning at exactly 50 Hz. Solar and wind connect through inverters and store none. Trip a power plant, push renewables higher, and watch the frequency fall faster and deeper, until the grid sheds load or collapses. Then let a grid-forming battery fight back.

Who saves the grid?

The physics in one equation

2·Hsys · dΔf/dt = ΔPgov + ΔPbattery + ΔPshed − ΔPloss − D·Δf

Inertia (H)

The first line of defence. It decides how fast frequency falls in the first second (RoCoF = ΔP·f₀ / 2H). Inverter-based renewables contribute zero, so Hsys = Hsync·(1 − RES share).

Grid-following battery

Measures frequency with a PLL, waits for detection, then injects power. Strong, but ~250 ms late, and in a low-inertia grid the damage is done by then.

Grid-forming battery (VSM)

Behaves like a voltage source with a virtual rotor. It responds to RoCoF instantly (synthetic inertia), so it slows the fall before the frequency has dropped far.
