# Video script: "The Inertia Cliff" (≈70 s, 1080×1350 portrait or 1920×1080)

**Format:** screen recording of the live demo plus voice-over (or on-screen captions, since 80 % of LinkedIn video is watched muted).
**Tools:** OBS Studio or Windows `Win + Alt + R` (Xbox Game Bar) to record; CapCut / Clipchamp to add captions and zooms.
**Tip:** set browser zoom to 125 % and hide the bookmarks bar before recording.

| # | Time | On screen | Voice-over / caption |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0–5 s | Close-up of the spinning turbine icons, then the "Trip a power plant" button is clicked and one unit turns red | **"This power plant just tripped. Watch what happens to the grid."** |
| 2 | 5–15 s | "Classic grid" preset (10 % renewables). Frequency dips gently and recovers. | "In a traditional grid, spinning generators store kinetic energy. It's called inertia, and it slows the fall." |
| 3 | 15–27 s | Drag the renewable slider to 90 %. Turbines become solar panels. Click **Trip**: red curve dives, rotors visibly slow, KPI badge shows **ROCOF TRIP RISK**. | "Solar and wind connect through inverters. They store zero inertia. Same trip, and the frequency now falls nine times faster." |
| 4 | 27–37 s | Scroll to **The inertia cliff** chart and hover along the red curve. | "Sweep renewables from 0 to 95 % and you find the cliff, the point where the grid starts shedding customers." |
| 5 | 37–50 s | Back to the frequency chart: orange (grid-following) vs. green (grid-forming) curves. Zoom in on the first second. | "Add a 1,500 MW battery. A grid-following battery waits to measure frequency, and by then it's too late. A grid-forming battery acts like a virtual synchronous machine and responds in milliseconds." |
| 6 | 50–60 s | KPI cards: red ❌, orange ❌, green ✅ **SECURE · 0 MW shed** | "Same battery. Same megawatts. Zero load shed. The only difference is the control." |
| 7 | 60–70 s | Quick pan over the README and `npm test` passing, then end card: **Inertia Lab · Abdullah Alruwaili · link in comments** | "It's open source and runs in your browser. Link in the comments. Go break a grid." |
